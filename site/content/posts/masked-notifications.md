Notifications are the most common way secrets come out: a preview on a lock screen, a name in a banner. TRYST's are designed to say nothing.

## The problem

Most apps fill notifications with names, photos and message previews, all visible on a lock screen to anyone glancing at your phone. Emails arrive with the company's name in the sender and subject line.

## How it works

- **In-app inbox:** tells you *what* happened, such as "You have a new match", never *who*.
- **Push alerts are off by default.** If you turn them on, they carry **no content at all**: your phone simply shows your own wording, up to 40 characters, which can't include links or the word TRYST. The default is "You have an update".
- **Codes and alerts by email** come from a neutral sender, with wording that never names TRYST or describes the service.
- **Billing** shows a neutral name on your bank statement, and renewal reminders come from the payment processor to the billing email you choose.

## Why it's different

Push alerts are sent **empty**, so even the push service that delivers them learns nothing about what's happening. Other apps let you hide previews; TRYST never sends them in the first place.

## What it doesn't do

- It can't stop someone who unlocks your phone from opening the app. Use your device's own lock and [quick exit](/blog/quick-exit-and-burn/).
- Turning push off means you'll only see updates when you open TRYST.
- Your bank statement still shows a payment, under a neutral name, if you subscribe to [TRYST+](/blog/tryst-plus-honest-pricing/).

## In short

Your lock screen says what you told it to. Nothing more.
