Losing your phone shouldn't mean losing your account. But recovery is also the favourite route for anyone trying to take over someone else's. TRYST's recovery is built for both facts.

## The problem

Most "forgot password" flows hand control to whoever can read your email or receive your texts. Support teams can often be talked into resetting an account. For an account that holds your secrets, that's the weak point.

## How it works

1. **Start recovery** with the email on your account. The answer looks the same whether or not an account exists.
2. **Face check**, then an **ID check with the same document** you verified with. A different document ends the attempt at once.
3. **A 24-hour wait.** Your account is told when recovery starts and again when the wait begins. **Any device still signed in can cancel it**, so if it isn't you, you stop it.
4. **After the wait**, every old passkey and session is revoked, and you have 15 minutes to add a new passkey on your new device. Then you sign in as normal.

It also unlocks an account frozen with the [emergency lock](/blog/emergency-lock/).

## Why it's different

There is **no staff override**. Nobody at TRYST can be persuaded, pressured or paid to recover your account. The only way back in is the same identity that opened it, plus time for the real owner to object. Your email address alone can never take over your account.

## What it doesn't do

- It isn't instant. The 24-hour wait is the protection.
- It needs the same ID document you verified with. If you've lost that too, you'll need a replacement of the same document.
- Your old devices' message history isn't recovered: [messages](/blog/end-to-end-encrypted-messages/) live on your devices.

## In short

Your face, your ID, a day to object, and no back door for anyone.
