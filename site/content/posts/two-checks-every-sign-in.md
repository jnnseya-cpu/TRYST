Most accounts are one leaked password away from being someone else's. On a service where the stakes are your relationships, your job and your reputation, that isn't good enough. So TRYST doesn't have passwords at all.

## The problem

Passwords get reused, guessed and phished. Text-message codes can be intercepted or read on a shared phone. Email codes are only as private as an inbox that a partner might also open. Every one of those routes lets someone who isn't you into your account.

## How it works

Every sign-in needs **two separate checks**, and the second always comes from something only you have.

- **On your phone:** your passkey (unlocked with your fingerprint, face or screen lock), then a short live face check that it's really you.
- **On a computer:** your passkey, then approval from your registered phone (you scan a code and confirm), or a second security key if you prefer.

Your email address proves who you are once, when you join. After that it can never sign you in or add a device. Five wrong attempts lock the account for 15 minutes, and you can see your sign-ins for the last 90 days, day by day, split by phone and computer, from your account.

## Why it's different

Most services treat a password plus an optional code as security. TRYST treats **two factors you physically control** as the minimum, on every device, every time. There is no "forgot password" link for someone else to abuse, and no code for a partner to read over your shoulder. Nobody at TRYST can sign in as you either: there is no staff override.

## What it doesn't do

- It can't stop someone who has your unlocked phone *and* your face from opening the app. If your phone is taken, use the [emergency lock](/blog/emergency-lock/).
- It doesn't hide that the app exists on your device. See [quick exit and Burn](/blog/quick-exit-and-burn/) for what you can do about that.
- If you lose every device, you'll go through [account recovery](/blog/account-recovery-without-a-back-door/), which takes 24 hours by design.

## In short

Two checks, every time, on every device. It takes a few seconds longer than a password. It means your account is yours.
