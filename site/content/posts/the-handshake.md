"It's a match" is where most apps start the awkwardness. On TRYST, a mutual interest starts a short, private decision first.

## The problem

Matching on most apps opens a chat immediately, before either person has thought about whether it's really a fit. Conversations stall, people feel obliged to reply, and an unwanted opening line is already in your inbox.

## How it works

When interest is mutual (you both sent intents, or one of you accepted the other's), TRYST opens a **handshake**.

- **Each of you gets your own brief:** their name, age, area, approximate distance, and what you share, such as what you're both looking for and interests in common. You see what overlaps, not their full answers.
- **Each of you decides:** accept or decline.
- **The conversation opens only when both of you accept.** Until then, nobody can message anybody.
- **If either declines, it closes.** Nobody is told who declined.

If one of you is in a [couple](/blog/couples/), both partners see the brief, and the couple's own veto rule decides their side: either partner can say no, and if they've chosen "both", both must say yes.

## Why it's different

The handshake puts **consent before contact**. Nobody lands in your messages uninvited, you never have to write a rejection, and each of you sees only what's relevant to the two of you. It turns "maybe" into a clear decision quickly, which is the point: meeting, not chatting forever.

## What it doesn't do

- It doesn't share your full profile answers, and it doesn't share theirs with you.
- It doesn't set up the meeting itself. Once you're talking, you arrange that in your [encrypted conversation](/blog/end-to-end-encrypted-messages/).
- It can't make anyone accept, and it won't tell you why they didn't.

## In short

Two yeses, then a conversation. Never one before the other.
