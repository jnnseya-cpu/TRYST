Being signed in proves you were there when you signed in. It doesn't prove you're the one holding the phone now. Step-up closes that gap.

## The problem

On most apps, anyone with your unlocked phone can do anything you can: add their own device, download your data, make you more visible. The account is only as private as the moment you looked away.

## How it works

For a handful of sensitive actions, TRYST asks for **both sign-in checks again**, inside your session. It takes a few seconds, and lasts five minutes:

- seeing your **devices**, adding one or removing one;
- **downloading your data**;
- making yourself **more visible**: loosening your [Discretion Policy](/blog/discretion-policy/), removing a [hidden area](/blog/hidden-areas/), clearing your [partner shield](/blog/partner-shield/) or coming back from [pause or vanish](/blog/pause-and-vanish/);
- getting a new emergency code.

Some actions **never** ask, because a delay could hurt you: Burn, Panic, reporting, blocking, cancelling a subscription and deleting your account.

Your **devices** list shows every passkey on your account, with its type and when it was added. You can't remove your only device by accident: add another first, or use [Burn](/blog/quick-exit-and-burn/).

## Why it's different

TRYST draws a deliberate line: **anything that exposes you needs proof it's you; anything that protects you never waits**. Many services ask for a password again before changing a password. TRYST asks again before every change that matters for discretion.

## What it doesn't do

- It doesn't ask for every action, only the sensitive ones. Ordinary use stays smooth.
- Within those five minutes, a second sensitive action won't ask again.
- It can't protect you if someone has both your phone and your face. That's what the [emergency lock](/blog/emergency-lock/) is for.

## In short

Protecting yourself is instant. Exposing yourself takes your fingerprint, or your face.
