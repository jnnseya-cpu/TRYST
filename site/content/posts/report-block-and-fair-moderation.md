Safety on a platform comes down to what happens after someone presses "report". Here's what happens on TRYST.

## The problem

On many services, reports vanish into a queue with no deadline, one person can remove an account on a hunch, and appeals go back to the same team that made the decision. Reporters worry they'll be identified, and wrongly removed members have no real way back.

## How it works

- **You choose what to share.** Your messages are end-to-end encrypted, so a report includes only the messages you select. We can't see anything else.
- **Your identity is protected.** The member you report is never told who reported them, and moderators see the report, not a profile of you.
- **Deadlines by severity.** Every report becomes a case with a deadline set by how serious it is, with the most urgent cases (suspected minors, image abuse, extortion and threats) dealt with first and fastest. Interim freezes can stop harm while a case is reviewed.
- **Two people for the most serious decision.** A permanent removal needs two different moderators to agree.
- **Independent appeals.** If you appeal a decision about your account, it's reviewed by a moderator who wasn't involved in the original decision.
- **Accountable records.** Every moderator action is recorded in a tamper-evident log. Moderators see photos only when they record a reason.

Confirmed child sexual exploitation is reported to the National Crime Agency. People who are permanently removed [can't open a new account](/blog/one-person-one-account/).

## Why it's different

It's moderation built like a fair process: **two-person rule** for the gravest outcome, **independent review** on appeal, and **evidence you control** rather than a company reading everyone's messages.

## What it doesn't do

- We can't act on messages you don't share with us, because we can't read them.
- Blocking is instant; a report takes review time, according to its deadline.
- Moderation can't undo harm that has already happened. If you're in danger, call 999.

## In short

Your evidence, your anonymity, two people for the hardest call, and a fresh pair of eyes on appeal.
