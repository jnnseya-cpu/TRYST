A conversation on TRYST is between you and one other person. Not us, not a hacker who breaks in, not anyone who gets hold of our database.

## The problem

Most messaging on connection apps is stored in readable form on the company's servers. A breach, a rogue insider or a court order can expose it. For conversations like these, that's an unacceptable risk.

## How it works

- A conversation opens **only after a mutual yes**, through [the handshake](/blog/the-handshake/) or [Tonight](/blog/tonight/).
- Each message is **encrypted on your device**, separately for every device that you and the other person use. Your private keys never leave your devices.
- TRYST's servers store and relay **only ciphertext** they can't decrypt.
- You choose how long messages are kept: **7, 30 or 90 days**. The shortest period either of you picks applies, and expired messages are deleted.
- **On-device safety checks** run on your phone, not on our servers: they warn you about common scam patterns, refuse to send messages that read as grooming or child exploitation, and freeze a conversation for review if they detect signs of extortion.
- **Block** closes the conversation for both of you and hides you from each other everywhere. They aren't told why.

## Why it's different

Plenty of services say "secure messaging" and mean "encrypted on the way to our server". TRYST means **end-to-end**: the server is a postbox for sealed envelopes. Combined with short retention and safety checks that run on your device, you get privacy without giving up protection from scams and abuse.

## What it doesn't do

- It can't stop the other person from taking a screenshot or photo of their screen.
- Your messages live on your devices. If you lose all your devices, your message history goes with them.
- If you report a conversation, you choose which messages to share with our safety team. That's the only way we ever see any of it.

## In short

Sealed envelopes, short memories, and safety checks that never send your words to us.
