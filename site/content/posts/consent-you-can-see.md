A profile on TRYST says things about your sex life. UK law treats that as special category data, and so do we.

## The problem

Most services bury consent inside their Terms: agree to everything or leave. You can't see what you agreed to, when, or how to take it back. And "withdrawing" often means an email to support and a wait.

## How it works

- **Separate from the Terms.** Before you appear on introductions or Tonight, we ask specifically for your consent to process what your profile says about your sex life. It's a separate tick, with plain words, not a clause in the Terms.
- **Biometric consent** is asked before any face check, separately again.
- **A sealed history.** Every consent you give or withdraw is added to a record that can't be edited: each entry is linked to the one before it, so any change would break the chain. We check those chains continuously, and our on-call team is alerted if one ever fails.
- **See it all.** Your account shows each consent, whether it's given now, and the full history with dates and the wording version you agreed to.
- **Withdraw in one tap.** Withdraw consent to processing your profile and you leave introductions and Tonight **immediately**. Your account stays.

## Why it's different

Most consent is a checkbox you never see again. On TRYST it's a **living record you can read**, protected against tampering, and **reversible on the spot**. That's what "freely given" should mean.

## What it doesn't do

- Withdrawing consent stops future processing. It doesn't delete your account. For that, see [download or delete for good](/blog/download-or-delete-for-good/).
- Some processing doesn't rest on consent, such as keeping a permanent removal on record. Our privacy notice lists each one.
- Withdrawing consent for discovery means people can't find you, by design.

## In short

Asked plainly, recorded honestly, withdrawn instantly.
