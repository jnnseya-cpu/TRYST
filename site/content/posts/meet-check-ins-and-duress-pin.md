Meeting someone new should come with a plan for when things don't go as hoped. TRYST builds that plan into the meet itself.

## The problem

The usual advice is "tell a friend where you're going". That depends on the friend paying attention at the right time, and it means telling someone about a meeting you might want to keep private.

## How it works

1. **Propose a time window** in your conversation. The other person confirms. The place and any door details stay inside your [encrypted messages](/blog/end-to-end-encrypted-messages/); TRYST never receives the venue.
2. **Turn on check-ins** for that meet: every 15 minutes to 3 hours, with 10 minutes' grace.
3. **Choose two PINs:** your check-in PIN, and a different **duress PIN**.
4. **Choose who's told** if you miss a check-in: a trusted contact (by email at launch, for this meet only), our 24/7 safety line, or both.

If you miss a check-in, your trusted contact is alerted first, then the safety line, staffed around the clock by a professional alarm-receiving centre. Entering the **duress PIN gives exactly the same reply as a normal check-in**, so anyone looking over your shoulder sees nothing unusual, but escalation starts at once. When you're done, end the meet with **"home safe"**. Your trusted contact's details are deleted when the meet ends.

**Panic** is a separate control, available at any time.

## Why it's different

The **duress PIN** is built for the moment when you can't openly ask for help. The **timers run on our servers**, not your phone, so a dead battery or a phone taken from you still triggers the alert. Your trusted contact learns only your alias and the time window, not who you're meeting.

## What it doesn't do

- It isn't an emergency service. If you're in danger, call 999.
- The safety line can only act on what it knows: your alias, the time window and any information you've chosen to share.
- At launch, trusted contacts are reached by email, not text message.

## In short

A plan for the evening, a quiet way to ask for help, and people awake to answer.
