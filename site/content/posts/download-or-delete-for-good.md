Leaving a service should be as clean as joining it. On TRYST, you can take everything with you, or leave for good.

## The problem

"Delete account" on many platforms means a flag in a database, with your data still sitting in backups for months or years. Data breaches have exposed people who thought they'd deleted their accounts long before.

## How it works

**Download**

- One button gives you everything we hold about you: profile, photos, introductions, intents, consents, settings and conversation details.
- Because messages are [end-to-end encrypted](/blog/end-to-end-encrypted-messages/), their content is on your devices, not ours; the download includes what we hold about each conversation.
- It needs [both sign-in checks](/blog/step-up-and-your-devices/), so nobody with your unlocked phone can take a copy.

**Delete**

- Type "delete my account" to confirm. No step-up, no waiting, no persuading.
- Your photos are locked with a key unique to you. **That key is destroyed first**, so every copy of your photos becomes unreadable at once, including in backups. Copies of the key in key backups are gone within seven days.
- Then every part of TRYST removes the rest of your records straight away. Older copies in our encrypted database backups age out within 30 days, and if a backup is ever restored, your deletion is applied again before anything goes live.
- You receive a **signed completion proof**: a document, verifiable with our published key, listing what each part erased and any exception kept.
- A live subscription ends; inside the first 14 days you're refunded for the days you didn't use.

## Why it's different

**Cryptographic erasure** means deleting your photos doesn't depend on finding every copy: without the key, the copies are noise. A **deletion ledger** makes sure a restored backup can't bring you back. And the **signed proof** turns "trust us" into something you can check.

## What it doesn't do

- If you were permanently removed, a one-way code of your ID stays on a block list so you can't rejoin. The proof says so plainly.
- It can't delete messages already on the other person's device.
- Records other than photos can remain in encrypted database backups for up to 30 days before they age out; they're never restored into use.
- Deletion can't be undone.

## In short

Take it all, or leave for good, with proof either way.
