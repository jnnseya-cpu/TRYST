The people most likely to recognise you are the ones nearest your home and your office. Hidden areas keep them out of each other's view.

## The problem

Distance filters keep people far away from you visible but put everyone close by on display, which is exactly backwards for discretion. One recognisable face on the wrong screen is all it takes.

## How it works

- In your account, choose up to **four areas** from the list, such as your home district and your office district.
- **Anyone whose area is one of yours won't see you, and you won't see them.** It works both ways, on your introductions, on Tonight and everywhere else profiles appear.
- **Nobody is told.** The other person doesn't see a "hidden" profile or a gap; you simply aren't there.
- **Adding an area is instant. Removing one** shows you to more people, so it needs [both sign-in checks again](/blog/step-up-and-your-devices/).

## Why it's different

Most services can only hide you from people *you know*, if you know their details. Hidden areas protect you from people **you don't know you know**: the neighbour three doors down, the colleague from another floor. And because it's mutual, you won't stumble on them either.

## What it doesn't do

- It works on the areas members choose for their profiles, not live location. Someone from your hidden area who sets their profile somewhere else could still appear.
- It hides you from people *in* those areas. It doesn't stop someone from elsewhere who happens to know you; for a specific person, use the [partner shield](/blog/partner-shield/).
- Four areas is the limit, so choose the ones that matter most.

## In short

Not on your doorstep, not at your desk, and nobody the wiser.
