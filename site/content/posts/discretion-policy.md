Privacy settings on most apps are buried, easy to change by accident, and easy for someone else to change on your behalf. TRYST treats them as rules that protect you.

## The problem

If someone picks up your unlocked phone, they can often flip your profile to public in two taps. Settings change silently, nobody keeps a record, and you only find out when it's too late.

## How it works

Your **Discretion Policy** holds three choices, applied everywhere you can be seen:

| Setting | Options |
|---|---|
| Visibility | **Visible** to people on your introductions and Tonight, or **Incognito**: shown only to people you've shown interest in |
| Distance | About 5, 10, 25 or 50 km, applied both ways |
| Verification | The minimum verification level of anyone who can see you, applied both ways |

Two more controls sit alongside it: [hidden areas](/blog/hidden-areas/) and the [partner shield](/blog/partner-shield/).

**Tightening is instant.** Going incognito, shortening your distance or raising the verification floor takes effect immediately. **Loosening needs proof it's you.** Any change that would show you to more people needs [both sign-in checks again](/blog/step-up-and-your-devices/). Every version of your policy is kept, so you can see what changed and when.

## Why it's different

Most apps make privacy a toggle. TRYST makes it **one-way easy**: easy to hide, deliberate to reveal. That protects you from the most common real-world risk, which isn't a hacker but someone with access to your phone. And your rules aren't a filter applied once; they're checked **on every list and every photo request**.

## What it doesn't do

- Incognito doesn't hide you from people you've already shown interest in. That's the point of it.
- It can't stop someone you've matched with from remembering your profile.
- It doesn't hide that the app is installed. See [quick exit and Burn](/blog/quick-exit-and-burn/).

## In short

Hiding is one tap. Being seen by more people is a decision you confirm.
