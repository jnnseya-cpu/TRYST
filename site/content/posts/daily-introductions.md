Endless swiping rewards the apps, not you. TRYST gives you a short list each day, and tells you why each person is on it.

## The problem

Browsing apps make money from your attention: the longer you scroll, the better for them. Matches pile up, conversations go nowhere, and the people most in demand get buried in messages. Meanwhile you can't tell why you're being shown anyone.

## How it works

You tell us, from simple lists, who you are, who you'd like to meet, what you're looking for, what you'd never want, and a few interests. You confirm every answer, and every version is kept so you can see what changed.

Each day you get **up to 18 introductions**. Before anyone is ranked, **hard rules** run, and they are never traded off:

- you each want to meet the other's group;
- neither of you is shown something on the other's "never" list;
- blocks, [hidden areas](/blog/hidden-areas/), the [partner shield](/blog/partner-shield/) and both people's verification floors all apply;
- you're in the same city and within both people's chosen distance.

Then the rest are ranked on what you share: what you're looking for, your interests, how close you are and verification parity. Every card carries **two to four plain reasons**, such as "You want the same kind of thing" or "Close by". About one in seven cards is deliberately something a little different, so your list doesn't narrow forever. Pass on someone and they don't come back.

## Why it's different

- **Reasons on every card.** You always know why someone is there.
- **Limits are absolute.** A great score can never override a hard "no".
- **Nothing is paid placement.** No member can buy their way onto your list.
- **Short by design.** Eighteen considered introductions, not an endless feed.

## What it doesn't do

- It isn't a search engine: you can't browse every member, and nobody can browse you.
- The ranking uses only what you've confirmed and what you share with someone. It doesn't read your messages.
- It can't create chemistry. That part is still yours.
