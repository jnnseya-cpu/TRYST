You've arranged to meet. Someone arrives. Is it the person you've been talking to? The door check answers that in five seconds.

## The problem

Photos can be old or borrowed, and people can be persuaded to send someone else. Asking for a surname or a phone number to confirm identity gives away the very details you're trying to keep private.

## How it works

When you plan a meet, a **door secret** is created inside your [encrypted conversation](/blog/end-to-end-encrypted-messages/). TRYST's servers never see it. From that secret, **both phones work out the same three everyday words**, such as "amber, bridge, comet".

When you meet, one of you says the words, and the other checks they match what's on their own screen. If they match, the person in front of you holds the conversation you've been having. If they don't, you don't have to explain anything: you leave, and you can raise the alarm with your [check-ins](/blog/meet-check-ins-and-duress-pin/).

## Why it's different

It's **identity confirmation without identification**. No names, numbers or documents change hands, and nothing about it passes through our servers. The words are short, easy to say quietly, and different for every meet.

## What it doesn't do

- It confirms the person has access to your conversation. It can't tell you whether someone else has their phone or is with them.
- It doesn't replace your judgement. If something feels wrong, leave.
- Both of you need your phones with you.

## In short

Three words, said quietly at the door. If they match, it's them.
